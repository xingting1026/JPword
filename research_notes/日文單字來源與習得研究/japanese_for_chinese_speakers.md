# Japanese vocabulary acquisition by Chinese-L1 (esp. Taiwanese) learners, and what the writing system implies for a vocab app

Scope note: Findings below come from Japanese-language SLA / psycholinguistics literature (日本語教育, 広島大学 教育学研究科, NINJAL, 台灣日語教育學報) plus a few English-language papers. Where I could only see an abstract or a secondary citation, I say so. Original terms (同形異義語, 語種, 特殊拍, etc.) are kept beside translations. Each subtopic ends with a one-line design implication.

---

## Key Question 1 — Cognate advantage: how much easier are Sino-Japanese (漢語) words, what are the 日中同形語 categories, and how do "false friends" (同形異義語) cause errors?

### Takeaway
Roughly 30% of the 20,000 most frequent Japanese words are 日中同形同義語 that a Chinese reader can understand by sight without ever studying them (a "built-in lexicon"), but about 1 in 5–6 同形語 (≈5% of the top 20,000) are 同形類義 / 同形異義 "要注意語" where L1 meaning is automatically activated and produces errors; textbooks for Chinese learners typically gloss these with a single Chinese equivalent and fail to flag the divergence.

### Cited Findings
- **Quantities (松下・陳・王・陳 2020, 『日本語教育』177, pp. 62–76, 「日中対照漢字語データベースの開発と応用」)**: Among the top 20,000 Japanese words (VDRJ frequency), 50% are 漢語 (Sino-Japanese / on-reading words). 70% of 漢語 (35% of all words) are 同形語 (isographic with modern Chinese); 30% of 漢語 (15% of all) have no modern-Chinese cognate (非同形語). Of the 7,074 同形語, 82% (29% of all words, 58% of 漢語) are 同形同義 (same form, same meaning); the other 18% — "one out of 5 or 6 isographic words" — are 同形類義 or 同形異義, "words learners need to be especially careful with". The classification used 6 semantic-correspondence types based on 文化庁 (1978) and 三浦 (1984). — [Abstract, 日本語教育 177](https://www.nkg.or.jp/gakkaishi/.assets/177_5abstract.pdf)
- **"Built-in lexicon" (松下達彦 2018/2019, NINJAL 第四回学習者コーパス・ワークショップ 発表資料「日本語語彙習得に関わる普遍性と個別性―漢字をめぐる問題を中心に―」pp. 48–55)**: "上位2万語のうち、30％にあたる約6千語がいわゆる日中同形同義語で、5%に相当する約千語が同形類義あるいは同形異義の要注意語である。中国語母語学習者の多くは学習しなくても日中同形同義語を目で見て理解できるが、学習しなければ音で聞いても理解できない語が多い。" (≈6,000 of the top 20,000 words are 同形同義; ≈1,000 are 同形類義/同形異義 caution words; most Chinese-L1 learners can understand the 同形同義 words by sight without study, but cannot understand them by ear unless studied.) He calls this a very large "built-in lexicon" (citing Dalton 2004). — [NINJAL PDF](https://www2.ninjal.ac.jp/jll/lsaj/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/869b469213df7c0f6deaad728b97a6b9.pdf)
- **Vocabulary-size gap by L1 (松下・佐藤・笹尾・田島・橋本 2021, 『日本語教育』178, pp. 139–153, 「日本語の語彙量と漢字力―第一言語と学習期間の影響―」, 2021 日本語教育学会 Best Paper)**: VSTRJ-50K (Vocabulary Size Test for Reading Japanese, items sampled from top 50,000 words of VDRJ) on 971 examinees: Chinese-L1 students' estimated reading vocabulary size averaged **over 30,000 words**, non-kanji-background learners **under 20,000**, Japanese-L1 university students over 40,000. DIF analysis by L1 showed "Japanese L2 students of Chinese background had a much easier time understanding Chinese-origin words, while non-kanji background students had an easier time understanding Western-origin words. In addition, Korean L1 students had an easier time understanding Western-origin words than Chinese-origin words." Correlation between vocabulary size (VSTRJ-50K) and kanji conversion (KCT) r = .82 (n = 446). — [Best Paper summary PDF](https://www.nkg.or.jp/.assets/2021bestpaper_b.pdf)
- **Definition and classification of 同形語**:
  - 大河内康憲 (1992)「日本語と中国語の同形語」『日本語と中国語の対照研究論文集（下）』くろしお出版, pp. 179–216. Definition: 「いずれがいずれを借用したかを問わず、双方同じ漢字（簡体字は問わない）で表記されるものを同形語とよぶ」 (regardless of borrowing direction, words written with the same kanji in both languages). Excludes single characters used in both on/kun (山, 人, 大, 小); targets 2-character (sometimes 3+) 字音語 such as 文化, 経済, 克服, 普通. — as summarised in [趙乾・上原徳子 2017, 宮崎大学教育学部紀要 人文科学 89, pp. 35–45](https://miyazaki-u.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/5272.1/files/89_jinbun-p35.pdf)
  - 潘鈞 (1995)「中日同形詞詞义差異原因浅析」『日語学習与研究』3, pp. 19–23: three necessary conditions — same kanji orthography (ignoring 簡体/繁体 and okurigana), shared origin/historical link, currently used in both languages. — same source
  - **文化庁 (1978)『中国語と対応する漢語』大蔵省印刷局**: selected ≈2,000 of 10,000 words for teaching Japanese to Chinese speakers and classified them as **S語** (same meaning in both; same), **O語** (meanings partially overlap; overlapping), **D語** (completely different meaning; different), **N語** (exists only in Japanese; nothing). — same source
  - The popular 3-way labels 同形同義 / 同形類義 / 同形異義 correspond to S / O / D. 王永全・小玉新次郎・許昌福 (2007)『日中同形異義語辞典』東方書店 marks each sense as [同] / [近] / [異]. — same source
- **Scale of 同形語**: 許雪華 (2014)「日中同形語の量的分析」『関西大学 或問 WAKUMON』26, pp. 113–122 reports "日中同形語は12,000語以上存在している" (over 12,000 Sino-Japanese homographs). — cited in [趙・上原 2017](https://miyazaki-u.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/5272.1/files/89_jinbun-p35.pdf)
- **Textbook evidence of the false-friend problem (趙乾・上原徳子 2017)**: In 『新編日本語』1–4 (the most widely used university textbook in China, ≥370,000 copies), 同形語 are >28% of all vocabulary (1,492 of 5,190 words). Of those, S語 >70%, O語 ≈17% (257 words), D語 the fewest (131). The textbook glosses each word only as 「漢字 + ルビ + 品詞 − 中国語訳語」 showing only the sense used in that lesson; e.g. 「勝負（名）- 胜负」 omits the Japanese-only sense "to compete / a match", and 「関心（名）- 关心」 does not warn that Chinese 关心 also means "to care for someone". Authors conclude 「中国人日本語学習者は母語の干渉を受けやすい…同形語の日中区別をうまく説明できていなければ、中上級に至るまで同形語を把握することは難しい」 (Chinese learners are easily subject to L1 interference; without explicit contrast they will not master these words even at intermediate/advanced level). Example O語 listed: 真実, 自尊, 姿勢, 監督, 親子, 設置, 完了, 関心, 応用, 一周, 一環, 冊, 作業, 高度, 検討, 機械, 処理, 十分, 失敗, 百姓, 反省, 本心, 不明, 娘, 湯, 勉強-type words. — [趙・上原 2017 PDF](https://miyazaki-u.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/5272.1/files/89_jinbun-p35.pdf)
- **Automatic L1 activation (松下 2021, JASLA symposium paper 「漢字学習と語彙学習はどうあるべきか」 pp. 112–117, NINJAL repository)**: 「基本義や共起成分が異なっている場合は違いを意識的に克服する必要がある。漢字を見た瞬間に中国語知識は自動的に活性化するからである（ただし，習熟度が高まるにつれてL2知識はL1から独立していく（茅本, 2000 など））。」 (Where core meaning or collocates differ, the difference must be overcome consciously, because Chinese knowledge is activated automatically the instant the kanji is seen; however L2 knowledge becomes more independent of L1 as proficiency rises — 茅本百合子 2000, 『教育心理学研究』48, pp. 315–322). — [NINJAL repository PDF](https://repository.ninjal.ac.jp/record/2000019/files/Matsushita2021_kanji-vocab-learning.pdf)
- **Psycholinguistic processing of 同形異義語**: 当銘盛之 (2016) 博士論文「中国語を母語とする日本語学習者における中日同形異義語の処理過程：中日2言語間の音韻類似性と意味関連性を操作した実験的検討」広島大学 (甲第7048号). Experimental manipulation of cross-language phonological similarity and semantic relatedness. (Only metadata was retrievable.) — [Hiroshima Univ. repository](https://hiroshima.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/2004092)
- **Processing route depends on cognate status (邱學瑾 Chiu Hsueh-Chin 2003, 「台湾人日本語学習者における日本語漢字熟語の処理過程：日・中2言語間の同根語と非同根語の比較」『広島大学大学院教育学研究科紀要 第二部』51, pp. 357–365)**: In semantic-decision tasks, Taiwanese learners accessed the meaning of **cognates (同根語)** directly from orthography or via Chinese phonology — "Japanese phonology did not contribute"; for **non-cognates** Japanese phonological processing became relevant alongside orthography. — [Hiroshima Univ. repository](https://hiroshima.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/2031020)
- **Sentence context modulates it (徐婕 Xu Jie 2018, 「中国人学習者の日本語文の読みにおける漢字単語の処理過程：口頭翻訳課題を用いた実験的検討」『広島大学大学院教育学研究科紀要 第二部』67, pp. 191–199)**: Moving-window oral translation: facilitation from orthographic similarity for low-phonological-similarity words in high-constraint sentences; facilitation from phonological similarity for low-orthographic-similarity words in low-constraint sentences. — [Hiroshima Univ. repository](https://hiroshima.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/2031680)
- **L1 gloss helps meaning retention for Chinese learners (王舒茵, 『日本語教育』191, 「語注とL1対応語彙化が読解における付随的語彙学習に及ぼす影響―中国語を母語とする日本語学習者を対象に―」)**: 65 intermediate Chinese learners; L1 (Chinese) glosses significantly improved immediate and 4-week-delayed meaning recall; words with no one-to-one Chinese equivalent (L1-non-lexicalized) showed more partial but more durable retention. — [Abstract PDF](https://www.nkg.or.jp/gakkaishi/.assets/191_04abstract.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a Chinese-L1 user, the meaning of 同形同義 words is essentially pre-known; drilling "kanji → meaning" for them is wasted repetitions. What they lack is the **reading (音形)** and the **collocations/usage**.
- The 18% of 同形語 that are O/D-type should be a separately flagged category with an explicit Chinese-contrast note (what the Japanese word means vs. what the same characters mean in Mandarin), since a single-equivalent gloss reproduces the textbook failure documented by 趙・上原.
- Both a frequency database (VDRJ) and a cognate-type database (日中対照漢字語データベース, JKVC) already exist in Matsushita's work and could in principle drive automatic tagging of S/O/D status.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the full text of 松下ほか 2020 to list the 6 correspondence types or to obtain the word-by-word database (it is distributed via Matsushita's site www17408ui.sakura.ne.jp/tatsum/, which refused connection during this session).
- No large-scale error-rate statistics for specific false friends (勉強, 手紙, 大丈夫, 娘, 走る, 老婆) were found; 趙・上原 lists examples from a textbook but reports no learner error data. 走る/走 is explicitly *excluded* from 同形語 by 趙・上原 because of okurigana.
- 当銘 2016's actual results (RT/error patterns for 同形異義語) were not retrievable beyond the title.

**Design implication for the app:** Tag every kanji word by cognate type (S/O/D/N or 同形同義/類義/異義/非同形); for S-type words skip or de-weight meaning recall and make the kana reading the recall target; for O/D-type words show an explicit 「中文≠日文」 contrast card and keep meaning recall active.

## Key Question 2 — The "reading problem": do Chinese-L1 learners under-attend to phonological form, and should the app test the kana reading rather than the meaning?

### Takeaway
Yes. Multiple studies show Chinese-L1 learners access kanji-word meaning orthographically (often via Chinese phonology) and score markedly lower on the same words when they are presented aurally; researchers explicitly recommend "audio-focused" vocabulary learning and simultaneous text+sound processing for this group.

### Cited Findings
- **Reading vs. listening knowledge diverges by L1 (松下 2018/2019)**: 「L1が中国語であれば，『読む・書く』の語彙力が『聞く･話す』の語彙力よりも高く出ることが予想されるが，逆に非漢字圏学習者では後者の方が高くなる」 (Chinese-L1 learners' reading/writing vocabulary exceeds their listening/speaking vocabulary; the reverse for non-kanji-background learners). He therefore named his test the "Vocabulary Size Test *for Reading* Japanese". — [NINJAL PDF](https://www2.ninjal.ac.jp/jll/lsaj/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/869b469213df7c0f6deaad728b97a6b9.pdf)
- **Direct measurement of the gap (佐藤尚子・松下達彦・笹尾洋介・田島ますみ・橋本美香 2020, 「学術共通語彙に関する音声知識と文字知識の違い―中国語および韓国語を第一言語とする日本語学習者に焦点を当てて―」『第22回専門日本語教育学会研究討論会誌』pp. 26–27)**: Audio and written versions of the Japanese Common Academic Word Test (JCAWT) were given to Chinese-L1 and Korean-L1 learners; both groups scored higher on the written version, but **the written–audio gap was larger for Chinese-L1**. Matsushita predicts other L1s would score higher on the audio version. — cited in [松下 2021 JASLA paper](https://repository.ninjal.ac.jp/record/2000019/files/Matsushita2021_kanji-vocab-learning.pdf)
- **Auditory vocabulary size test for Chinese learners (彭悦・梁震・笹尾洋介, 『日本語教育』185, 「日中バイリンガルの音声版日本語語彙サイズテストの開発と検証」)**: 118 Chinese learners of wide proficiency; Rasch-validated. Compared to prior written-vocabulary data for Chinese speakers: (a) the correlation between auditory and written knowledge grows in the order 和語 < 漢語 < 外来語; (b) 「漢語については文字提示よりも音声提示の方が難しい一方で，和語・外来語は音声提示の方が易しくなる」 — for 漢語, auditory presentation is *harder* than written, whereas for 和語 and 外来語 auditory is *easier*; (c) under auditory presentation there was no significant difficulty difference among 和語/漢語/外来語. Authors: "These findings suggest the importance of learning focusing on auditory vocabulary." — [Abstract PDF](https://www.nkg.or.jp/gakkaishi/.assets/185_7abstract.pdf)
- **Orthographic route for cognates (邱學瑾 2003)**: for Japanese–Chinese cognates, Taiwanese learners' meaning access came from orthography or Chinese phonology; Japanese phonology did not contribute. — [Hiroshima Univ. repository](https://hiroshima.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/2031020)
- **茅本百合子 (2000)「日本語を学習する中国語母語話者の漢字の認知―上級者・超上級者の心内辞書における音韻情報処理」『教育心理学研究』48, pp. 315–322**: cited by Matsushita as evidence that L2 (Japanese) phonological representations become more independent of L1 as proficiency rises. — cited in [松下 2021](https://repository.ninjal.ac.jp/record/2000019/files/Matsushita2021_kanji-vocab-learning.pdf)
- **Related Hiroshima-school findings (search-result summaries, not individually verified)**: orthographic similarity between Chinese and Japanese facilitates, phonological similarity inhibits, kanji-word RTs for Chinese learners; low-proficiency learners show stronger L1 phonological activation, advanced learners stronger L2 phonological activation; "for Chinese learners of Japanese, discrimination between orthographic information is easier than discrimination between phonological information of the two languages". — [Hiroshima Univ. Bulletin series](https://ir.lib.hiroshima-u.ac.jp/journals/BullGradSchEducHU-Part2/i/67/item/46785); [ICU repository record](https://icu.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/4621)
- **Kanji knowledge and reading knowledge are tightly linked, but kanji is a sound-less script (松下 2021)**: "漢字は文字が音を直接に表さないため" tests often use kana as a pseudo-phonological form; KCT (kana+context → choose kanji) and VSTRJ-50K (kanji word → choose meaning) correlate r = .82, implying "形だけを覚えて意味がわからない，あるいはその逆といったことが少なく，両方を覚えることで知識として安定する" — but he adds the caveat that **reading vs. listening vocabulary develops differently depending on L1**. — [松下 2021](https://repository.ninjal.ac.jp/record/2000019/files/Matsushita2021_kanji-vocab-learning.pdf)
- **Matsushita's explicit recommendations for Chinese-L1 learners (松下 2021, §3.2)**: (1) because core meanings and collocations often match, "文字と音声の同時処理による付随的語彙学習は効果的かもしれない" — simultaneous text+audio incidental learning (scripted extensive listening, subtitled films, same-topic reading+listening; "ふりがな付き多読も効果があるかもしれない"); (2) where meanings differ, the difference must be consciously overcome; (3) a 「語彙学習先行モジュール」 (vocabulary-first module, 松下 2002, 2005) that front-loads vocabulary using L1 kanji knowledge. Summary: 「漢字語の音声インプットを教師も学習者もより意識して，音声と文字の語彙知識をスパイラルで高めていく」 (both teachers and learners should attend more to **audio input of kanji words**). — same source
- **Learning pronunciation alongside meaning aids meaning retention (Aoyama, K. 2005, MA thesis BYU, "Using a Diglot Reader to Teach Kanji: The Effects of Audio and Romaji on the Acquisition of Kanji Vocabulary")**: 1-week retention .73 for both meaning and pronunciation; learners who acquired both meaning and pronunciation retained kanji meaning better than those learning meaning alone. (Non-Chinese learners.) — [BYU ScholarsArchive](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/etd/538)

### Inferences
- For Chinese-L1 users the binding constraint on a kanji word is the Japanese sound form, not the meaning. Recall prompts should therefore be **kanji/meaning → kana reading (typed or spoken)** and **audio → meaning/kanji**, rather than kanji → meaning.
- Because 漢語 are harder by ear than by eye for this group, the app should include a pure-audio modality for 漢語 specifically; conversely 和語/外来語 are easier by ear, so audio can be the *introduction* channel for those.
- Multi-reading kanji (音読み vs 訓読み) is the specific failure point, so disambiguating readings in context (e.g., 上昇/のぼる) is worth a dedicated exercise type (Matsushita suggests 音読み二字漢語 ⇄ 和語 synonym paraphrase drills, using already-known words).

### Gaps
- I found no study that directly measured "percentage of kanji words a Chinese learner knows the meaning of but cannot read aloud"; the evidence is indirect (written vs. audio test gaps).
- Tamaoka Katsuo's own Chinese-learner papers were not retrievable in this session (only Hiroshima-school/Nagoya-affiliated studies); a direct Tamaoka citation is missing.

**Design implication for the app:** Make the kana reading (or audio recognition) the primary recall target for kanji words; treat "kanji → meaning" as a low-weight check only for O/D-type cognates and 非同形語.

## Key Question 3 — Are kana-only native words (和語) and katakana loanwords (外来語) the hardest for Chinese learners?

### Takeaway
Yes on a relative basis: Rasch/DIF analyses show 漢語 are relatively easy and 外来語 relatively hard for Chinese-L1 learners (the opposite of English/other-L1 learners), survey data show 90% of Chinese-speaking learners have had trouble with katakana words in daily life and 64% cite "cannot guess the meaning", and English knowledge helps only when it is L1-level.

### Cited Findings
- **語種 difficulty by L1 (松下・佐藤・笹尾・田島・橋本 2021, 『日本語教育』178; also 松下ほか 2016)**: Rasch item difficulty by word type and L1: 「中国語L1には『相対的に』漢語は易しく外来語は難しい，韓国語L1とその他L1にとって外来語が易しく，日本語L1にとって和語が易しい」 (for Chinese L1, relatively: 漢語 easy, 外来語 hard; for Korean and other L1s 外来語 easy; for Japanese L1 和語 easy). Matsushita attributes this to "漢字を媒介とするL1知識の転移の有無" (presence/absence of kanji-mediated L1 transfer). — [松下 2021 JASLA](https://repository.ninjal.ac.jp/record/2000019/files/Matsushita2021_kanji-vocab-learning.pdf); [NINJAL 2018 PDF](https://www2.ninjal.ac.jp/jll/lsaj/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/869b469213df7c0f6deaad728b97a6b9.pdf)
- **Learner survey (陣内正敬 2008「日本語学習者のカタカナ語意識とカタカナ語教育」『言語と文化』11, pp. 47–60, 関西学院大学; n = 479 learners at 198 institutions, 49.7% Chinese speakers)**, as reported by 望月通子 (2012): (1) katakana reading/writing is harder for Chinese speakers than for Korean speakers (who have a phonographic script); (2) 77.8% of all learners had trouble with katakana words in daily life — **90% of Chinese speakers** vs. under 70% of Korean speakers; (3) Chinese speakers' top difficulties: 「意味推測不能」64.1%, 「発音の違い」60.5%, 「辞書不掲載」55.6%, 「表記の困難さ」52.5%; (5) 71.8% of Chinese speakers (vs. 45.0% Korean) asked to be taught katakana words "more properly". — [望月通子 2012「基本語化を考慮したカタカナ外来語の学習と教材開発」『関西大学外国語学部紀要』6, pp. 1–16](https://kansai-u.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/10178/files/KU-1100-20120300-01.pdf)
- **Why katakana words are hard (望月 2012, §3, synthesising prior work)**: (1) 発音のズレ — foreign sounds squeezed into CV moras and 5 vowels (cut→カット, food→フード); (2) 表記のゆれ (メール/メイル, メイン/メーン); (3) 意味のズレ — narrower/shifted meanings (アウトレット); (4) 和製英語 (スマート, ナイター, ハローワーク; 4 types per 柴崎・玉岡・高取 2007『日本語科学』21, pp. 89–110); (5) 短縮語 (リモコン, パソコン, コンビニ); (6) 混種語 (生ビール, アイロン台); (7) verbalisation (サボる, メモる, アピールする); (8) non-loan katakana uses (onomatopoeia, バラ); (9) 和語・漢語・外来語 near-synonyms (ケース/場合, トラブル, メリット). A Chinese learner quoted: 「『ヨーロッパ』という単語は一番時間をかかりました。私の一番苦手の小さい『ッ』と伸ばすの発音…特に、英語はその英語発音が分かれば、日本語で覚えるのは簡単になりました」 (ヨーロッパ took the longest because of the small ッ and the long vowel; English words whose pronunciation I know are easier). Post-course test on 300 academic katakana words: mean 80.0 immediately, **60.4 two months later** (SD 16.2) — "定着率が低く，ばらつきが大きい". — same source
- **Katakana increasingly "basic" vocabulary**: NINJAL (2005) magazine survey — loanwords increased 3.5× between 1956 and 1994; 33.8% of distinct words in 1994 magazines were 外来語; abstract loanwords such as タイプ, システム, バランス, ケース, トラブル have become 基本語 (金愛蘭 2011). — same source
- **English knowledge: L1 vs. L2 (松下 2021)**: 「英語知識の日本語の外来語学習への応用については検証が必要だが，現時点では，英語L1知識は活用できるが，英語L2知識のプラスは少ないと考えている」 (English-L1 knowledge can be exploited, but the benefit of English-as-L2 knowledge appears small). — [松下 2021](https://repository.ninjal.ac.jp/record/2000019/files/Matsushita2021_kanji-vocab-learning.pdf)
- **Auditory modality flips the ranking (彭・梁・笹尾, 『日本語教育』185)**: with audio presentation, 和語 and 外来語 were *easier* than with written presentation, and the three 語種 did not differ significantly in auditory difficulty — i.e., the 漢語 advantage of Chinese learners is largely a *written* advantage. — [Abstract PDF](https://www.nkg.or.jp/gakkaishi/.assets/185_7abstract.pdf)
- **和製漢語 are only partly guessable**: a 『日本語教育』 study of Chinese speakers with no Japanese learning found about 30% (約3割) of 和製漢語 (Japan-coined two-character words) could be correctly inferred from Chinese knowledge. — [search-result summary of nkg abstract; primary not retrieved](https://www.nkg.or.jp/gakkaishi/.assets/180_1abstract.pdf)
- **Genre matters**: 「日本語の学術系語彙…の7割以上は漢語である。これは文芸語彙…の7割以上が和語であるのと対照的である」 (over 70% of academic vocabulary is 漢語; over 70% of literary vocabulary is 和語). — [松下 2018 NINJAL PDF](https://www2.ninjal.ac.jp/jll/lsaj/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/869b469213df7c0f6deaad728b97a6b9.pdf)

### Inferences
- For Chinese-L1 users the app's marginal value is concentrated in 和語 (especially kana-only adverbs/verbs) and 外来語; 漢語 S-type words need only reading practice.
- Katakana words need (a) the source word shown (to exploit whatever English the user has), (b) explicit flags for 和製英語 and 短縮語, and (c) spelling practice focused on ッ and ー, which Chinese learners name as their hardest point.
- Because retention of katakana words decays quickly (80 → 60 in two months in 望月's course), spaced review intervals for 外来語 should be shorter than for 漢語.

### Gaps
- No study was found that gives per-word accuracy for specific kana-only adverbs (やはり, ほとんど, しばらく) by Chinese learners; the 和語-hard claim rests on aggregate Rasch difficulty and on 日本語教育 185's modality comparison.
- No quantitative study linking Taiwanese learners' *measured* English proficiency to katakana-word scores was found (only learner self-reports and Matsushita's judgement).

**Design implication for the app:** Allocate review effort inversely to the cognate advantage — prioritise 和語 and 外来語 for Chinese-L1 users, show the English/other source form and 和製英語 flag for katakana words, and schedule katakana items for more frequent review.

## Key Question 4 — Frequency-based word selection: VDRJ, coverage thresholds, vocabulary-size tests, JLPT counts, and "kanji words come for free"

### Takeaway
Matsushita's VDRJ (BCCWJ-based, 33 million tokens) shows Japanese needs ~9,000–12,000 lemmas for 95% coverage and 20,000–24,000 for 98% (vs. ≈6,400 / 12,800–14,400 lemma-equivalents in English), but kana + the top 1,000 kanji already cover 95.1% of tokens — and for Chinese-L1 learners about 30% of the top 20,000 words are pre-known by sight. The commonly quoted JLPT N-level word counts descend from the pre-2010 出題基準; the new test publishes no lists.

### Cited Findings
- **VDRJ (松下達彦 2011「日本語を読むための語彙データベース（VDRJ）Ver. 1.1」)**: built from the BCCWJ 2009 monitor (books + Yahoo!知恵袋), 33 million running words, with separate rankings for international students, general learners and general written Japanese. — [Kramer 2023, Journal of Extensive Reading 11(2)](https://jalt-publications.org/content/index.php/jer/article/view/1221); [松下 2021 refs](https://repository.ninjal.ac.jp/record/2000019/files/Matsushita2021_kanji-vocab-learning.pdf)
- **Coverage thresholds (Matsushita, T. 2012, *In what order should learners learn Japanese vocabulary? A corpus-based approach*, PhD thesis, Victoria University of Wellington, p. 155)**: 「日本語では，95%のカバー率を達成するのに9,000～12,000語，98％を達成するのに20,000～24,000語も必要である」; specifically **95% requires the top 9,446 語彙素 (lemmas)**. For English, 95% ≈ 4,000 word families ≈ 6,400 lemmas; 98% ≈ 8,000–9,000 families ≈ 12,800–14,400 lemmas (Nation 2006; Milton 2009 ×1.6). — [松下 2018 NINJAL](https://www2.ninjal.ac.jp/jll/lsaj/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/869b469213df7c0f6deaad728b97a6b9.pdf); [松下 2021](https://repository.ninjal.ac.jp/record/2000019/files/Matsushita2021_kanji-vocab-learning.pdf)
- **Kanji-based coverage (Matsushita 2012 Table 6-1, reproduced as 表1 in 松下 2018)**: on 33,153,137 tokens — hiragana-only words 59.7%; + katakana-only 63.6%; + top 100 kanji 73.8%; + top 300 kanji **82.6%**; + top 500 88.0%; + top 1,000 **95.1%**; + top 1,500 98.1%; + top 2,000 99.2%. 「漢字上位1000字（ほぼ教育漢字に相当）で95％に到達する」. Caveat: knowing the kanji ≠ knowing the word (秋+季節 → 秋季 is guessable; 名+人 → 名人 is not), so a database of 漢字語の意味的透明度 (semantic transparency) is needed. — same NINJAL PDF
- **Reading-coverage threshold used in Japanese research**: 小森・三國・近藤 (2004)「文章理解を促進する語彙知識の量的側面―既知語率の閾値探索の試み―」『日本語教育』125, pp. 83–92 (vocabulary explains r² = .47 of reading comprehension; threshold search for known-word rate). — cited in [松下 2018](https://www2.ninjal.ac.jp/jll/lsaj/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/869b469213df7c0f6deaad728b97a6b9.pdf). Kramer (2023) adopts 98% rather than 95% as the comprehension standard, citing Hu & Nation 2000; Laufer & Ravenhorst-Kalovski 2010; Schmitt, Jiang & Grabe 2011. — [Kramer 2023](https://jalt-publications.org/content/index.php/jer/article/view/1221)
- **Vocabulary-size tests**: VSTRJ-50K (「日本語を読むための語彙量テスト」, 40-min MC, items sampled from VDRJ top 50,000; 田島ほか 2015; 佐藤ほか 2017; 松下 2012a) and KCT (Kanji Conversion Test, 20-min MC, top 2,000 kanji of CDJ; 松下ほか 2021). A Japanese Vocabulary Levels Test for extensive reading was built on Matsushita's (2012a) General Learners' list (Kramer 2023). An **auditory** Japanese–Chinese bilingual VST exists (彭・梁・笹尾, 日本語教育 185). JCAWT (academic words) has audio and written versions at http://www17408ui.sakura.ne.jp/tatsum/webtest.html#JCAWT. — [Best Paper summary](https://www.nkg.or.jp/.assets/2021bestpaper_b.pdf); [Kramer 2023](https://jalt-publications.org/content/index.php/jer/article/view/1221); [日本語教育 185 abstract](https://www.nkg.or.jp/gakkaishi/.assets/185_7abstract.pdf)
- **JLPT word counts — what is actually documented**: 松下 (2021) states the *old* JLPT defined 3級 (≈ current N4) as 「漢字（300字程度）・語彙（1,500語程度）を習得」 and 2級 (≈ N2) as 「漢字（1,000字程度）・語彙（6,000語程度）を習得」. — [松下 2021](https://repository.ninjal.ac.jp/record/2000019/files/Matsushita2021_kanji-vocab-learning.pdf). The old test ran from 1984 with levels 1級–4級; the 2010 revision added a level (N1–N5), moved to scaled scores and Can-do statements, and the JPF page on the new test mentions no vocabulary counts or lists. — [文化庁 report page](https://www.bunka.go.jp/tokei_hakusho_shuppan/tokeichosa/nihongokyoiku_suishin/nihongokyoiku_tenkai/hokokusho/2_4/); [JPF 日本語教育通信 2010-11](https://www.jpf.go.jp/j/project/japanese/teach/tsushin/news/201011.html). Third-party sites repeat N5≈800, N4≈1,500, N3≈3,750, N2≈6,000, N1≈10,000 and note that the Japan Foundation/JEES "have never published official vocabulary lists" for the new test. — [Migaku blog (secondary)](https://migaku.com/blog/japanese/jlpt-vocabulary-lists)
- **Kanji frequency vs. old-JLPT level mismatch (松下 2021 表1)**: of the top 1,000 kanji by frequency, 172 were classified 1級 (advanced) by the old JLPT (e.g., 離, 瞬, 魔), and 義・態・郎 are in the top 300; conversely many high-frequency words use low-frequency kanji that are usually written in kana anyway (駄目, 奴, 嘘, 即ち, 是非). Matsushita: distinguish receptive from productive goals; some words "理解さえできれば書く際にはひらがなやカタカナで済む". — [松下 2021](https://repository.ninjal.ac.jp/record/2000019/files/Matsushita2021_kanji-vocab-learning.pdf)
- **Study-time curve (松下ほか 2021)**: average study time per word/kanji is long for beginners, shortest at intermediate, longer again at advanced (low-frequency items); L2 learners need >2,000 study hours to match Japanese-L1 KCT scores. — [Best Paper summary](https://www.nkg.or.jp/.assets/2021bestpaper_b.pdf)
- **"Kanji words for free" for Chinese learners**: see KQ1 — ≈30% of top-20,000 words (≈6,000) are 同形同義 and understood by sight without study; Chinese-L1 reading vocabulary averages >30,000 vs. <20,000 for non-kanji learners. — [松下 2018](https://www2.ninjal.ac.jp/jll/lsaj/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/869b469213df7c0f6deaad728b97a6b9.pdf); [松下ほか 2021](https://www.nkg.or.jp/.assets/2021bestpaper_b.pdf)
- **Chinese-L1 learners write more 外来語 as they advance**: longitudinal composition data show 外来語 use increasing with study history among Chinese-L1 learners, with changes in semantic domains. — [search-result summary; primary not retrieved](https://repository.ninjal.ac.jp/record/2000275/files/papers2701.pdf)

### Inferences
- A Chinese-L1 word list should be built by taking a frequency ranking (VDRJ or similar) and *subtracting* S-type 同形語 from the "learn meaning" queue while *keeping* them in the "learn reading" queue; the effective new-meaning load for 95% coverage then drops from ≈9,400 to roughly 6,500–7,000 words (9,446 minus ≈30% S-type), though this is my arithmetic, not a published figure.
- JLPT N-level counts are useful only as a rough progression label; the N3 ≈ 3,750 figure in particular has no official origin (N3 was inserted between old 3級 and 2級 in 2010).

### Gaps
- Could not access Matsushita's website (connection refused) to confirm the current VDRJ version, licence, or the 日中対照漢字語データベース download.
- Did not locate the primary 『日本語能力試験出題基準【改訂版】』(2002) text for the 4級 ≈ 800 words / 100 kanji and 1級 ≈ 10,000 words / 2,000 kanji figures; they are widely cited but I only verified the 3級/2級 figures via Matsushita.
- No published figure for "words needed for 95% coverage *after* removing cognates for Chinese learners".

**Design implication for the app:** Drive ordering by corpus frequency (VDRJ-style), label items with JLPT levels only as a soft guide, and compute a per-user "effective load" by excluding S-type 漢語 from meaning-learning while keeping them for reading practice.

## Key Question 5 — Pitch accent and phonology: does Mandarin/Taiwanese tone help or hurt, and what about special moras (長音・促音・撥音)?

### Takeaway
Tonal L1 (Mandarin) gives a *perceptual* advantage for Japanese pitch accent (Mandarin listeners without Japanese discriminate accent natively or better), but cue-weighting differs from native Japanese, and special-mora duration control (促音/長音) remains a persistent production and spelling problem for Taiwanese learners even after they can perceive the contrast.

### Cited Findings
- **Wiener, S., & Goss, S. (2019). Second and third language learners' sensitivity to Japanese pitch accent is additive: An information-based model of pitch perception. *Studies in Second Language Acquisition*, 41(4), 897–910. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263119000068** — Six groups (n = 15 each) including L1-Mandarin with/without L3 Japanese. "L1-Mandarin listeners without Japanese experience discriminated pitch accent with nativelike abilities. L1-Mandarin + L3-Japanese listeners discriminated pitch accent significantly more accurately than L1-Japanese listeners." Even L1-English + L2-Mandarin listeners matched L1-Japanese accuracy. Sensitivity is modelled as a function of "pitch's informativeness across all languages a listener speaks". — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/second-and-third-language-learners-sensitivity-to-japanese-pitch-accent-is-additive/DD55DD75A260E90EBC312A93C4ADDEBE)
- **Liu, Yue. "The Effect of Fo Contour Interruption by Geminate Consonants on the Perception of Japanese Pitch Accent Categories: A Comparison between Native Japanese and Native Chinese Speakers." *Sophia Linguistica* (year/issue not shown on page)** — Both Japanese and Mandarin listeners use F0 maximum as the primary cue for accent presence/location; but Japanese listeners exploit the *falling* F0 slope and the geminate (促音) F0 interruption facilitates pitch-change perception for them, whereas Chinese learners "repurposed their L1-sensitivity to the *rising* F0 slope" and did not use the interruption cue the same way. — [Sophia Linguistica page](https://dept.sophia.ac.jp/is/solific/en/?p=3231)
- **鮎澤孝子 et al. — Chinese-L1 perception of Tokyo accent**: search-result summaries indicate 鮎澤's group ran Tokyo-accent listening tests across L1 groups and that Chinese (tonal) speakers perceived Japanese pitch accent better than Korean speakers, with lexical knowledge affecting accuracy. I could not open the primary PDF (NINJAL 98.8p57.pdf returned 404) — **treat as unverified**. — [KAKEN project record](https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/grant/KAKENHI-PROJECT-23652116); [NINJAL link (404 at time of access)](https://www2.ninjal.ac.jp/jalic/group5/98.8p57.pdf)
- **Taiwanese learners' 促音・長音 production (洪心怡 Hung Hsin-Yi 2015,「台灣人初級日語學習者的促音與長音之時間構造的分析」『台灣日語教育學報』24, pp. 29–50, 高雄第一科技大學)**: Acoustic measurement with the ratio-measures method: (1) beginners *can* distinguish 促音/非促音 and 長音/短音 and produce different durations, 「但尚未達到日語促音、長音該有的拍長時間」 (but do not reach the mora durations Japanese requires); (2) they use the same duration-control strategy for 促音 and 長音, giving both similar temporal structure; (3) no mora-timing compensation effect was found, 「初級日語學習者仍然將日語的拍當作音節在發音」 (they still produce Japanese moras as syllables). — [台灣日語教育學報 PDF](https://www.taiwanjapanese.url.tw/GAKUHO/24.files/No24-B02.pdf)
- **Toda Takako on 特殊拍 acquisition (国際交流基金『日本語教育通信』「日本語・日本語教育を研究する 第31回 日本語特殊拍の習得に関する研究」; Toda 2003 *Second Language Speech Perception and Production*, University Press of America; 戸田 2003, 2004a/b, 2006a/b)**: learners develop "learner-unique criteria" for distinguishing 促音・長音・撥音 that differ from native speakers'; two error types — deletion (切って→切て) and insertion (来て→来って); effective instruction requires diagnosing whether the error source is phonological knowledge, auditory discrimination, or articulation. — [JPF page](https://www.jpf.go.jp/j/project/japanese/teach/tsushin/research/031.html)
- **Learner self-report linking special moras to katakana spelling difficulty**: see 望月 2012 quote (ヨーロッパ hardest because of 小さい「ッ」 and 伸ばす発音) and 陣内 2008: 52.5% of Chinese speakers cite 「表記の困難さ」 for katakana words. — [望月 2012](https://kansai-u.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/10178/files/KU-1100-20120300-01.pdf)
- **Taiwanese learners: no correlation between study length and 促音 acquisition** (individual variation dominates) — search-result summary of a Taiwanese study; primary not retrieved. — [search summary; Osaka Univ. dissertation listing](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/70776/30108_Dissertation.pdf)

### Inferences
- Showing pitch accent is low-cost and high-yield for Mandarin/Taiwanese users: they can *hear* the pattern natively, so a visual accent mark (e.g., ⓪①② or a high/low line) is likely to be used rather than ignored; the risk is in production and in cue weighting, so audio playback should accompany the mark.
- Special-mora errors directly surface as kana spelling errors (dropped/inserted っ, ー, ん). Kana-typing recall will therefore catch the phonological problem that multiple-choice recognition hides.
- Because learners treat ッ and ー with the same timing strategy, items minimally contrasting them (e.g., カット/カート, きて/きって/きいて) are an efficient drill format.

### Gaps
- I could not obtain per-pattern accuracy rates (平板/頭高/中高/尾高) for Taiwanese listeners; the Tsukuba Tokyo-accent listening-test paper and the Osaka dissertation on 台湾語 speakers were not readable in this session.
- No study found on whether Taiwanese Hokkien (台語) tone, as opposed to Mandarin, changes the picture.
- "Non-native speakers pay less attention to pitch accent when speaking, so it does not improve with proficiency" appeared in a search snippet (Taiwan-Mandarin prosody study on labs.sonicfield.org) but the host could not be resolved; unverified.

**Design implication for the app:** Display pitch accent with audio for every headword (Mandarin users can exploit it), and make 促音・長音・撥音 explicit in typed-kana recall with minimal-pair distractors, since these are the persistent production/spelling errors.

## Key Question 6 — Script presentation: kanji, kana, or both when prompting; furigana dependence / "kanji crutch"

### Takeaway
Furigana demonstrably aids comprehension and inference for learners, and Matsushita explicitly flags "how effective furigana is for incidental learning of kanji readings, and how much furigana to give at which level" as an *open* research question; for Chinese learners the risk is specific — the kanji alone already yields meaning, so a kanji-only prompt tests nothing new, while a kana-only prompt forces retrieval of the unknown sound form.

### Cited Findings
- **Furigana improves lexical inference (Palmer, Joy A. 2012. "The Effect of Furigana on Lexical Inferencing of Unknown Kanji Words." MA thesis, Brigham Young University)**: think-aloud with 16 target kanji words; "the group with furigana made more correct inferences than the control group" and perceived the kanji as easier. — [BYU ScholarsArchive](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/etd/3235)
- **Furigana shortens kanji reading latency (Japanese psychology, 『心理学研究』50(2))**: hypothesis "that the presence of appropriate furigana would shorten the search time required for reading a kanji in on or kun" was supported; latency and recall probability depended on furigana, reading-style instruction, and each kanji's customary reading. — [J-STAGE 心理学研究 50(2) contents](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/browse/jjpsy/50/2/_contents/-char/ja)
- **Furigana effect differs by script background (search-result summary, primary not identified)**: "furigana did not considerably influence learners of Japanese with Kanji backgrounds in the recognition of Kanji characters, but learners with Non-Kanji backgrounds were more likely to activate phonological representations and the phonologically mediated route was more likely to be dominant when furigana was present." — [search summary; source list included J-STAGE LET 39 and Bunkyo Univ. repository](https://bunkyo.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/2000111) — **unverified**
- **Matsushita's position (松下 2021)**: for Chinese-L1 learners, "ふりがな付き多読も効果があるかもしれない" (furigana-assisted extensive reading may be effective) as part of simultaneous text+sound processing; but in his conclusion: 「ふりがなは漢字の読みの付随的語彙学習にどの程度効果的なのか，どのレベルの学習者にどの程度のふりがなつきテキストを与えるのが効果的か，など，まだまだ考えるべきことは多い」 (how effective furigana is for incidental learning of readings, and how much furigana to give at what level, remain open). — [松下 2021](https://repository.ninjal.ac.jp/record/2000019/files/Matsushita2021_kanji-vocab-learning.pdf)
- **Kana as pseudo-phonology in testing (松下 2021 §2.1)**: because kanji do not encode sound, Japanese vocab tests routinely use kana as a stand-in for the spoken form (kana → kanji conversion, kanji → kana reading); the KCT (kana+context → kanji) was found to be *less affected by L1* than the kanji-word → meaning test (VSTRJ-50K), i.e., presenting kana neutralises the Chinese reader's orthographic shortcut. — [松下 2018 §3.1](https://www2.ninjal.ac.jp/jll/lsaj/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/869b469213df7c0f6deaad728b97a6b9.pdf); [松下 2021](https://repository.ninjal.ac.jp/record/2000019/files/Matsushita2021_kanji-vocab-learning.pdf)
- **Orthographic route for cognates (邱學瑾 2003)** and **automatic L1 activation on seeing kanji (松下 2021, citing 茅本 2000)** — see KQ1/KQ2: a kanji prompt immediately delivers meaning via Chinese for a Taiwanese learner. — [Hiroshima](https://hiroshima.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/2031020)
- **Both forms matter, bound through the kanji image (松下 2021 §2.5)**: 「一つの字の音と訓が漢字のイメージを媒介に結合していることが重要」 — e.g., ジョウショウ(上昇) and ショウシン(昇進) should be linked to のぼる via the image of 昇; without this link each 音読み word is memorised separately and the item count explodes; also helps listening because 漢字音 have many homophones (上 ~ 場, 状; 昇 ~ 賞, 章). — [松下 2021](https://repository.ninjal.ac.jp/record/2000019/files/Matsushita2021_kanji-vocab-learning.pdf)
- **Words usually written in kana**: high-frequency words whose kanji are rare are normally written in kana (駄目, 奴, 嘘, 即ち, 是非) — "理解さえできれば書く際にはひらがなやカタカナで済む". — same source

### Inferences
- For Chinese-L1 users, the *answer side* of a card should always include both kanji and kana (plus audio); the *prompt side* should vary: kanji-only prompt → recall kana reading; kana-only or audio prompt → recall kanji + meaning. Kanji+furigana together on the prompt gives away the only thing the learner needs to retrieve.
- "Kanji crutch" is best mitigated not by hiding kanji permanently (it is the user's strongest asset) but by never letting furigana appear *before* a reading attempt, and by training 音/訓 links (上昇 ⇄ のぼる) rather than isolated readings.
- For 和語 habitually written in kana (やはり, ほとんど, しばらく, 駄目→だめ), present kana as the canonical form and show the kanji only as reference, matching real usage.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled study showing that displaying kanji with furigana *suppresses* learning of readings ("kanji crutch") in Chinese-L1 learners specifically; the mechanism is inferred from the orthographic-route findings and Matsushita's open question.
- The "furigana effect differs by kanji background" study could not be pinned to a verified citation.

**Design implication for the app:** Never show furigana on the prompt side for kanji words; prompt with kanji → type kana (or audio → kanji/meaning), reveal kanji+kana+accent+audio on the answer side, and present habitually-kana 和語 in kana as the canonical form.
